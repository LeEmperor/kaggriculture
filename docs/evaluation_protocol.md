# Evaluation Protocol

Status: **Implemented**. Refines the game plan's [Phase 6](kaggriculture_gameplan.md)
(strategy platform and baseline opponent population) and its
[Phase 7](kaggriculture_gameplan.md) dataset-splits, reported-statistics, and
champion-promotion sections. It sets no policy the game plan does not; where it is more
specific — exact split sizes, exact thresholds — those numbers are this document's to own,
and changing them means editing this file and re-recording every artifact that cited it.

Vocabulary: [`glossary.md`](glossary.md). Sequencing: the "Work plan and status" section
of [`ocaml_migration_decisions.md`](ocaml_migration_decisions.md), where this is step 11.

## What this document is for

Once search starts, every number produced becomes a claim about a policy, and the two
ways such claims go wrong are both procedural rather than technical: measuring on seeds
the policy was tuned on, and moving the bar after seeing the result. The defences are the
same two in every empirical discipline — hold out data, and predeclare the decision rule —
so both are fixed here, before there is a champion to be tempted about.

## Seed splits

Three immutable, disjoint sets, generated by `tools/seed_splits.py` and checked in under
`experiments/seeds/`:

| Split | Seeds | Used for |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `training` | 1024 | search, tuning, and any exploratory measurement; may be looked at without limit |
| `validation` | 512 | champion-promotion decisions, and nothing else |
| `holdout` | 512 | milestone reports; never used to choose between candidates |

The derivation is a SHA-256 counter stream per split (`kaggriculture/seeds/v1/<split>/<n>`),
mapped into `[1000, 2^31 - 2]`, with splits filled in the order `holdout`, `validation`,
`training` so that growing `training` later cannot perturb the two sets that gate
promotion. `python3 -m tools.seed_splits verify` re-derives all three and fails if a
checked-in file differs, if a seed repeats, or if two splits overlap;
`tests/test_seed_splits.py` runs that check on every test run.

Seeds start at 1000 because 0–89 are already the golden-vector and Phase 5 benchmark
development seeds. A number appearing in an evaluation artifact is therefore never one a
policy was debugged on.

**Common random numbers.** Every entrant plays every opponent on every seed of the split
in *both* seat orders. A difference between two entrants is then a difference between the
entrants, not between the seeds or the seats they drew.

## The entrant population

The population is heterogeneous by construction. All eight baselines are native OCaml
`Model.policy` functions in `baselines/`, not DSL families, because the DSL's game seam
is a one-tile, one-worker slice: `submission/vocabulary.py` and its OCaml mirrors expose
fifteen accessors about the tile the farmer stands on, and the action seam is
`PASS/DROP/HARVEST/WATER/PLANT` plus `BUY_SEED/SELL` with `hands` hard-coded empty. Four
of the six baselines the game plan names cannot be written inside that seam at all.

These are Tier 3 policies in the sense of
[`ocaml_migration_decisions.md`](ocaml_migration_decisions.md) Decision 3: research-only
opponents, never shipped, so they never need a Python implementation. The discipline that
document states — author in the DSL even for throwaway exploration — applies to *candidates*,
which may one day win and need to ship. A measuring stick is not a candidate. The
population is also what will tell us which accessors a real candidate needs, so widening
the DSL seam before running it would have been guessing.

An `entrants.json` entry is one of:

```json
"pass"                                                  the PASS baseline
{ "baseline": "crop-greedy" }                           a registry baseline
{ "family": "f.json", "candidate": "c.json",
  "label": "optional-name" }                            a DSL candidate, with its own family
```

`experiments/leagues/baseline_v1/entrants.json` is the checked-in Phase 6 population: the
eight baselines plus `monocrop-reorder-v1`, so the existing research policy is measured on
the same table as the sticks it has to beat.

### The baselines

| Id | Thesis |
| --- | --- |
| `pass` | does nothing; the floor every strategy must beat |
| `random-valid` | uniform over the *applicable* action menu, seeded from the game seed and seat |
| `crop-greedy` | short-cycle wheat on eight tiles near the shed, sold on sight |
| `premium-crop` | long-cycle melon on the same plot with the same single worker |
| `animal-focused` | cows on pasture, fed from a hand-run wheat plot with market top-ups |
| `animal-solo` | the same livestock engine with no crew, buying every unit of feed at market price |
| `expansion` | re-hires a full crew daily and buys land; wheat across the whole farm |
| `market-focused` | holds wheat bought into cheap supply and releases it into scarcity |

`crop-greedy`, `premium-crop`, and `expansion` are one engine at three settings
(`baselines/lib/field_policy.ml`), so the difference between them is a difference of
settings rather than of code — which is what makes the comparison between them mean
something, and is the shape the "randomized variants of each baseline" of Phase 6 will
need.

`random-valid` draws from the *applicable* menu rather than the full grammar. Upstream
silently no-ops an inapplicable action, so a policy sampling the raw grammar would spend
almost every turn doing nothing: a floor on the scoreboard without being a floor on
behaviour.

## Non-vacuity: the coverage gate

Every baseline declares the action shapes it exists to produce
(`baselines/lib/registry.ml`). An evaluation run tallies what each entrant actually
emitted, and a declared shape that never appeared fails the run — `kag_sim` exits
non-zero and names the shapes. This is the policy-side analogue of `tools/coverage.py`'s
gate on the differential population: a baseline that compiles, runs, and loses is
otherwise indistinguishable from one that emits PASS 719 times, since both produce a
number.

Declarations state what a baseline *structurally* produces, not what it once happened to
do. Two deliberate absences, each a finding rather than an omission:

- `random-valid` does not declare `HARVEST`. It reaches it — four times in sixteen full
  episodes — but only by accidentally watering the same tile on two consecutive days and
  then standing on it while ripe. The action space is not uniformly reachable, and a
  random agent cannot discover farming.
- `premium-crop` and `market-focused` do not declare the endgame `DROP`. A twelve-day
  melon finishes its last cycle around day 25 and a market-focused plot clears, so their
  units are carrying nothing on the final day and the dump phase correctly does nothing.

`baselines/test/baselines_test.ml` runs the gate over eight seeds in both seats on every
`dune test`. The gate is aggregate, not per game: shapes conditional on how an episode
happens to end prove nothing from a single game either way.

## Reported statistics

Every summary is computed from per-game records, not running totals, because medians,
percentiles, and intervals are not accumulable. `margin` is the subject's final money
minus the opponent's, measured from the subject's own side whichever seat it sat in.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `win_rate` | wins / games. The primary selection metric. |
| `score_rate` | (wins + ½ draws) / games. What the league table ranks on. |
| `win_rate_ci95` | Wilson score interval. Not the normal approximation, which is useless exactly where a promotion decision is most delicate — a rate near 0 or 1 — and which this one stays inside `[0, 1]` at. |
| `margin_mean`, `margin_median` | mean and median money differential |
| `margin_variance`, `margin_stddev`, `margin_stderr` | sample (n−1) dispersion |
| `margin_mean_ci95` | mean ± 1.96 · stderr |
| `margin_p5`, `margin_p95`, `margin_min`, `margin_max` | nearest-rank percentiles, so every reported value occurred in some game |
| `draws` | ties |
| `catastrophic_loss_rate` | fraction of games finishing below the configuration's `startingMoney` — the policy went backwards, defined from a rule constant rather than from a chosen number |
| `by_opponent`, `by_position` | the same summary per opponent and per seat |
| `worst_matchup` | the opponent with the lowest `score_rate` |
| `wall_seconds`, `cpu_seconds`, `turns_per_second`, `games`, `turns` | compute time and volume |

## The evaluation artifact

`kag_sim` produces the measurement; `tools/league.py` wraps it with everything needed to
decide later whether the measurement can be believed, and writes the result to
`experiments/results/leagues/` (gitignored — artifacts are reproducible from their
inputs). The shape is `experiments/evaluation.schema.json`:

```
kind             "league" | "evaluation"
project_commit   or "WORKTREE" when the tree is dirty
upstream_commit  from reference/upstream.lock.json
simulator        "ocaml-native"
build, machine, recorded_at, player_positions
seed_split       { name, version, file, sha256, seeds }
inputs           every file read, with sha256 digests
native           the kag_sim payload, verbatim
```

`evaluation.schema.json` is a sibling of `experiment.schema.json`, not a replacement:
that one describes a single pairwise comparison and requires a `policy_a` and a
`policy_b`, which a population measurement does not have. Pairwise experiment results
still go through the older schema.

`kag_sim evaluate --copies N` repeats an identical, deterministic workload; it exists so
the Phase 5 throughput benchmark can fill a worker pool, and that benchmark requires the
flat totals to scale linearly with it. The statistics must not, so they are computed over
one copy and the artifact says how many distinct games that was
(`native.statistics_over_games`). A 95% interval over fifty copies of the same twenty
games would report a seventh of its true width.

`seed_split.name` is the load-bearing field. A seed file outside the three immutable
splits is labelled `ad-hoc`, and the promotion check refuses such an artifact — which is
the entire reason for labelling it.

## Champion promotion

A candidate becomes champion only when **all** of the following hold, measured on the
`validation` split against the standing opponent population, with the incumbent measured
on the same seed file and the same population:

| Criterion | Threshold |
| --- | ---: |
| both artifacts measured on `validation`, same seeds file, same opponents file | required |
| `score_rate` against the population | ≥ 0.55 |
| `win_rate_ci95` lower bound | ≥ 0.50 |
| `margin_mean_ci95` lower bound | > 0 |
| `margin_mean_ci95` lower bound versus the incumbent's `margin_mean` | strictly greater |
| per-opponent `score_rate` drop against the incumbent | ≤ 0.10 for every opponent |
| `worst_matchup.score_rate` | ≥ 0.20 |
| `catastrophic_loss_rate` | ≤ 0.10 |

Running the rule:

```bash
python3 -m tools.league promote --challenger A.json --incumbent B.json
```

The thresholds live in `tools/league.py` (`PROMOTION`) as well as in the table above, and
the two must agree. Making the rule executable is the point: a predeclared confidence
rule that lives only in prose is one a tired person applies loosely at exactly the moment
it matters most.

Three of the criteria are not about being better on average, and each blocks a specific
failure the game plan warns about. The per-opponent regression limit is "does not
introduce a severe opponent-specific regression" — a candidate that gains overall by
collapsing against one opponent is a candidate that will collapse in the live ladder. The
`worst_matchup` floor is the same guard stated absolutely rather than relatively. The
catastrophic-loss ceiling rejects a high-variance candidate whose mean is carried by its
tail; `random-valid` finishes below its starting bankroll in 100% of games, which is what
that statistic is calibrated against.

Promotion does not retire the incumbent from the population. Previous champions stay in
`entrants.json`, which is the game plan's rule against cyclic forgetting.

## Commands

```bash
# Regenerate and verify the immutable splits
python3 -m tools.seed_splits verify

# The baseline league (round-robin, both seats, coverage gate on)
python3 -m tools.league run --split training --threads 8

# One candidate against the population
python3 -m tools.league evaluate --split validation \
  --family experiments/policies/monocrop_reorder/family.json \
  --candidate experiments/policies/monocrop_reorder/candidate_baseline.json

# The same, for a baseline
python3 -m tools.league evaluate --split training --baseline animal-focused

# Apply the promotion rule to two artifacts
python3 -m tools.league promote --challenger A.json --incumbent B.json

# The native layer directly, without provenance
dune exec --profile release fast_model/bin/kag_sim.exe -- league \
  --entrants experiments/leagues/baseline_v1/entrants.json \
  --seeds experiments/seeds/training.txt --threads 8
```

### What it says

**The ordering is a near-strict hierarchy.** 58 of the 72 directed pairings are decisive —
the stronger policy wins every one of 2048 games. Only four matchups are genuinely
contested: `animal-focused` vs `animal-solo` (0.553), and three around `expansion`, which
is the one entrant whose results depend on the seed rather than on the matchup.

**A hired crew is worth almost nothing.** `animal-focused` runs three hands growing feed;
`animal-solo` is the identical livestock engine with no crew, buying every unit of feed at
market price. Head to head the crew wins 0.553, with a **mean margin of $484 and a median
of $106 on a ~$30,000 bankroll** — 1.6%. Mean final money is $30,053 against $29,746. This
matters well beyond the baselines, because hands are the single most expensive thing the
DSL could be extended with; see [`dsl_seam_extension.md`](dsl_seam_extension.md).

**Seeds have little leverage, and seat order none.** Per-position win rates agree to within
0.006 for every entrant. The environment's randomness is weed spawns and shop unlocks, and
neither has much purchase on a deterministic strategy. The 512-seed validation split is
cheap insurance rather than statistical necessity *for policies of this kind* — a
stochastic or market-timing candidate could easily change that, which is why it is not
being shrunk.

**Doing nothing beats some of what is easy to write.** `pass` scores 0.176, ahead of
`random-valid` (0.000, which finishes at exactly $0 in 100% of games — the first hire of a
day costs $1, so a random agent can and does hire itself to bankruptcy) and it beats
`expansion` in 40.8% of their games.

**Aggressive expansion is a trap at these prices, and the reason is walking.** Land costs
$1000/$2000/$4000 while a full daily crew costs about $54 — the hire price is the Fibonacci
sequence in the day's hire count, so eight hands cost 1+1+2+3+5+8+13+21. Labour is nearly
free and land is not, but the binding cost is neither. Units respawn at the shed every
morning, so a tile in a bought quadrant costs most of a worker-day in travel: `expansion`
spent 65.8% of its unit-actions on MOVE and finished below its starting bankroll in 33.8%
of games. It ranks sixth of nine.

**The current research policy ranks seventh, and the DSL seam is why.**
`monocrop-reorder-v1` loses every game to the five policies above `expansion`, and beats
only `pass` and `random-valid`. Its action histogram is the diagnosis: **0.0% MOVE and
93.2% PASS**, against 30.0% MOVE for `premium-crop`. It cannot move, so it farms the single
tile it spawns on and idles for nine-tenths of the episode — not because the strategy is
badly tuned but because the vocabulary it is written in has no MOVE, no hands, and no
addressing for any tile but the one underfoot. No parameter search inside that seam closes
a gap to $30,053. What to widen, in what order, and what each tier costs is
[`dsl_seam_extension.md`](dsl_seam_extension.md).

### A score is relative to its population

Adding `animal-solo` to an eight-entrant league re-scored everyone: `premium-crop` fell
from 0.857 to 0.757 and `monocrop-reorder-v1` from 0.364 to 0.319, without either policy
changing a line. Nothing was wrong with either measurement; a score rate is defined against
an opponent set, and the opponent set changed.

This is why the promotion rule compares the digest of the opponents file as well as the
seeds file, and refuses a comparison across different populations. It is also why a league
table should be cited with its artifact rather than from memory: the number is only
meaningful alongside the population that produced it.

### What the population is not

Every entrant is deterministic and none was tuned, so the table measures strategy
archetypes, not the best achievable play of each. `animal-focused` leading should be read
as "livestock is the strongest of these shapes", not as a ceiling. The population's job is
to be a fixed, interpretable ruler that a searched candidate has to clear, and the game
plan's instruction — do not optimize only against the current best policy — is why all
eight baselines stay in `entrants.json` even though three of them lose to inaction.
